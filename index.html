The Problem

New role, new team,
no real picture yet.

When you start a new leadership role, you need to understand how the team thinks, where the business actually stands, and what the real priorities are. That normally takes months. First 60 gets you there in a day.

People perform

New teams tell new bosses what they think they want to hear. The real picture takes months to emerge through one-to-ones alone.

Alignment is assumed

Ask six people to describe how the business works and you'll get six different answers. Most leaders don't find this out until it causes a problem.

Decks don't diagnose

Strategy documents tell you what the team wants you to see. Team dynamics, competitive blind spots, and hidden tensions don't live in slides.

Discovery takes too long

Every week spent figuring things out is a week you're not setting direction. First 60 gives you both at the same time.

How it works

Every exercise has two purposes.

On the surface, the team is collaborating — reflecting, drawing, mapping, debating. Underneath, the new leader sees how the team thinks, where they agree, where they don't, and what they avoid.

The team feels heard. The leader gets a diagnostic read on the people, the culture, and the real state of the business.

The team thinks they're doing a strategy workshop. They are. But the new leader is also getting a read on their organisation that would normally take three months of one-to-ones.
The Sprint

Seven exercises across two sessions.

Session 1 — Build the Picture 3 hours
01

The Arc

Where we've been, where we are, where we're heading

The team maps the story of the business along a visual arc — key milestones, current position, future aspirations. Where have we been, where are we now, and where do we want to go?

The team thinks

"We're reflecting on our journey together and aligning on where we want to go."

The exec sees

Who's honest about the past vs who romanticises it. Who has a realistic view of the future vs who's in fantasy land.

~40 min
02

Draw Your Business

Sketch it out — simply and clearly

Each participant draws a simple diagram of how the business works — as if explaining it to an old friend on a sticky note. How does value flow? Who's involved? What's the shape of the thing? They will all draw different things. They always do.

The team thinks

"Fun exercise. Nice break from sticky notes and bullet points."

The exec sees

Six people, six different diagrams. Who puts customers at the centre? Who draws the org chart? Instant read on alignment — or lack of it.

~30 min
03

Competitive Landscape

Where do we stand — and do we agree?

The team maps their competitors and places the business on a positioning landscape. Who are the real threats? Where are the gaps? Where does the team think they sit?

The team thinks

"We're doing a competitive analysis to help the new boss understand our market."

The exec sees

Does the team even agree on who the competitors are? Are they delusional about their position? Where are the blind spots?

~35 min
04

Headwinds & Tailwinds

What's pushing us forward, what's holding us back

What's helping the business move forward? What's holding it back? The team maps both sides and the balance between them shows the real state of play.

The team thinks

"Standard strategy exercise. Let's map our strengths and challenges."

The exec sees

Who's brave enough to name real headwinds? Who only talks about tailwinds? That tells you who's political and who's useful.

~35 min
Session 2 — Confront & Commit 3 hours
05

Mean Tweets

Say the ugly stuff — then decide what to do about it

What would customers, competitors, or employees say about the business if they were being brutal? The team writes the criticisms, then plots them on a matrix to separate the noise from the real problems.

The team thinks

"This is fun. Good to get this stuff out in the open."

The exec sees

Every criticism the market has — surfaced by the team themselves, on day one.

~45 min
06

Lightning Round

Five tough questions, answered fast

Rapid-fire responses to five provocative questions: If I were a competitor, what move would I make? What unlikely alliance could change everything? What happens if nothing changes? What's the one no-regrets move? What are we too scared to say out loud?

The team thinks

"Quick-fire brainstorm. Some tough questions but that's the point."

The exec sees

What the team is actually thinking but hasn't said yet.

~35 min
07

How Might We Change

What needs to change, and when

The team identifies what needs to change across nine areas — priorities, skills, resources, marketing, sales, innovation, product, leadership support, and investment — then maps actions onto a quarterly plan.

The team thinks

"We're building a plan together."

The exec sees

Which areas the team prioritises, which they avoid, and a plan they built themselves — which they can now be held to.

~45 min
